## 0. The organisations are simulated

- **Roles, not data sources.** MCI, the Bank, the E-commerce company and the Credit bureau are roles. Every partner organisation is a generic simulated role: no real bank, e-commerce company or credit bureau is meant, and none is named. MCI is the only real organisation named. It hosts the grant, and in the scenario it is the lender.
- **No real company's data is used.** Every row comes from one public dataset: Kaggle's Home Credit Default Risk competition (2018), records of one consumer lender's applicants. No data of MCI or of any other company is used. No organisation supplied, reviewed or endorsed these results.
- **The column split is a framing.** Section 5 assigns one dataset's tables to the four roles. It illustrates which kind of member could hold which kind of data. Section 5.3 states, for every member, where the framing is weakest.
- **What the numbers mean.** Every number of this instance describes this assignment over this dataset: model quality, contributions, payouts, business metrics and credit tiers. None is a measurement of the value of a real organisation's data.
- **Shown everywhere.** The dashboard's banner says so on every page, extended for v2: all partner organisations are generic simulated roles over a public dataset, and no real company's data is used. The banner names the dataset, Kaggle Home Credit Default Risk (the student's review, change 4). The report figures say so in their captions (Steps 6 and 7).

**Names** (the naming rule, 2026-10-19). Every user-facing output of v2 uses these names: this document and the other v2 documents, the dashboard, the figures and the Phase 3 mapping. The thesis code keeps its slot names; `scenarios/mci_loan/v2/slots.json` maps them to these names, and is the only place the mapping is written.

## 5. Columns: every used column and its owner

Source: `scenarios/mci_loan/v2/rule2.py`, `results/mci_loan/v2/select_mci.json` and `results/mci_loan/v2/prepared/columns.json`. A test checks that every one of application_train's 122 columns is in exactly one group (`scenarios/mci_loan/v2/tests/test_v2.py`).

### 5.1 MCI (active): 8 columns, chosen on the training split

**Candidates.** 47 one-number columns from application_train:
- v1's 40 Bank candidates: application, income, employment, family, region, documents, and ratios derived from them;
- the 7 phone and contact columns, v1 MCI's: `FLAG_MOBIL`, `FLAG_EMP_PHONE`, `FLAG_WORK_PHONE`, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE`, `FLAG_PHONE`, `FLAG_EMAIL`, `DAYS_LAST_PHONE_CHANGE`.

**Procedure.** v1's (`scenarios/mci_loan/select_bank.py`), with one rule added:
- sequential forward selection, each step adding the candidate with the highest mean 5-fold CV macro-F1;
- HistGradientBoostingClassifier (max_iter 300, learning rate 0.08);
- on the training rows only, with weight of evidence fitted inside each fold;
- **added (the student approved it, section 1, change 1):** from step 2 on, a candidate whose addition changes no prediction in any fold is skipped.

The circuit takes exactly 8 active columns (`circuits/src/macro_f1_members_var.circom:57`).

| Step | Added | CV macro-F1 (training split) | Gain | Skipped as no-ops |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | `ANNUITY_TO_CREDIT` | 0.6092 | +0.6092 | – |
| 2 | `NAME_EDUCATION_TYPE` | 0.6151 | +0.0059 | `FLAG_MOBIL`, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE` |
| 3 | `DAYS_BIRTH` | 0.6192 | +0.0041 | `FLAG_MOBIL`, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE` |
| 4 | `REGION_RATING_CLIENT` | 0.6226 | +0.0033 | `FLAG_MOBIL`, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE` |
| 5 | `REGION_RATING_CLIENT_W_CITY` | 0.6270 | +0.0045 | `FLAG_MOBIL`, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE` |
| 6 | `DOC_COUNT` | 0.6267 | -0.0003 | `FLAG_MOBIL`, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE` |
| 7 | `OCCUPATION_TYPE` | 0.6274 | +0.0007 | `FLAG_MOBIL`, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE` |
| 8 | `EMPLOYED_TO_AGE` | 0.6304 | +0.0030 | `FLAG_MOBIL`, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE` |

**Why the rule.** v1's rule, run as it is (`results/mci_loan/v2/select_mci-literal.json`), picks 2 columns that change nothing: `FLAG_MOBIL` at step 6, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE` at step 7. The reasons:
- `FLAG_MOBIL` has one value on every training row.
- `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE`'s less common value is on 22 training rows. A split needs at least 20 rows on each side (the model's leaf minimum), and a fold's training part holds about 18 of them, so no fold's model splits on it.
- Adding either one reproduces the previous step's predictions in every fold, at every step from 2 on, and so the previous step's score exactly. It therefore wins any step where every informative candidate lowers the score.

Sources: `select_mci.json`, `twoValuedCandidates`, `model.min_samples_leaf` and `steps[].noOp`.

v1's own Bank selection had such a step (step 6, gain -0.0003; `results/mci_loan/select_bank.json`), but no candidate that changed nothing. With the rule, a step picks the best column that changes something, as v1's did.

The literal result: `ANNUITY_TO_CREDIT`, `NAME_EDUCATION_TYPE`, `DAYS_BIRTH`, `REGION_RATING_CLIENT`, `REGION_RATING_CLIENT_W_CITY`, `FLAG_MOBIL`, `FLAG_CONT_MOBILE`, `DOC_COUNT`. Two of MCI's 8 inputs would be dead: constant, or as good as constant. Both selections are recorded; v2 uses the rule's 8 (the student's review, change 1).

**No phone or contact column was selected.** MCI's 8 are application columns. At every step the best phone or contact column scored below the column chosen. It came closest at step 7, with `DAYS_LAST_PHONE_CHANGE`, 0.0018 below (`select_mci.json`, `steps[].all`).

The 8 are v1's Bank columns, in the same order: the 7 added candidates changed no step's choice. So C1b (MCI trained alone, outside the federation) is v1's C1b. BASELINE (U_A) is not v1's: it reuses MCI's bottom model as trained in the federation, now with other partners (`contracts/offchain/scoring.mjs`), and retrains only the head.

### 5.3 Why each member holds what it holds, and where the framing is weakest

**MCI (active): the lender's application data and the label.**
- **The framing.** MCI grants short-term credit to its subscribers. When a subscriber applies, MCI collects an application form (income, employment, family, housing, documents, the credit asked for) and knows the subscriber's contact details. TARGET is read as repayment of the MCI-issued credit.
- **Where it is weakest.** In the data, TARGET is payment difficulties on a consumer loan of the dataset's lender, cash or revolving, not on operator credit. The amounts (`AMT_CREDIT`, `AMT_ANNUITY`, `AMT_GOODS_PRICE`) are that loan's, larger and longer than a 1- or 4-month operator credit. The phone and contact flags are what the applicant told the lender, not network data.
- **No telecom behaviour** (the student's review, change 5). The public data contains no telecom behaviour: no usage, no top-ups, no bills. 'MCI alone' is therefore a lender's application model, not an operator's. In a pilot, MCI's real telecom data would join MCI's own side: more columns for MCI's bottom model, which the federation, the proofs and the settlement do not change.

**Bank: installments_payments and credit_card_balance.**
- **The framing.** A bank holds its customers' repayment history on instalment credit (due day and amount against paid day and amount) and their card balances, limits, drawings and payments. It is a bank's own data.
- **Where it is weakest.** Both tables are the dataset lender's records of its own previous credits to the same applicant. A real bank would hold its own customers' records, which would cover the subscribers only in part. Here the coverage is that lender's: 759 cohort applicants have no instalment row and 11,276 no card month.

**E-commerce company: POS_CASH_balance and previous_application.**
- **The framing.** An e-commerce company that finances purchases in instalments (point-of-sale, buy-now-pay-later) sees each plan's monthly status and days past due, and every earlier financing application with its amount, down payment, term and decision.
- **Where it is weakest.** Both tables mix purchase financing with cash and revolving loans: an e-commerce company would not see an applicant's cash-loan applications. Like the Bank's, they are the dataset lender's own previous credits. Its measured contribution is therefore that of instalment-purchase data with this structure *and this strength*, not an estimate of what a real e-commerce company's data would add.

**Credit bureau: unchanged from v1.** bureau and bureau_balance (credits reported by other lenders), the three external scores and the enquiry counts are a bureau's own data.

**The Bank and the E-commerce company overlap by construction.** installments_payments, credit_card_balance and POS_CASH_balance are monthly or per-payment views of the same previous credits; previous_application lists them. Their columns are therefore correlated, and each one's leave-one-out contribution is measured given the other. The Talmud claims (section 10) inherit this: a partner whose data the other partner largely repeats has a small claim.

### 5.5 Age stays, sex is excluded

- **`CODE_GENDER` is excluded**, as in v1. Sex is a prohibited basis for credit decisions under widely used fair-lending rules, with no exception for scoring systems. One example is the US Equal Credit Opportunity Act's Regulation B (12 CFR 1002.2(z) and 1002.6(b)(1)). It is in no member's columns, so no model sees it.
- **`DAYS_BIRTH` stays.** It is MCI's column 3, picked by forward selection (section 5.1). v1's three reasons hold:
  1. The same rules allow age in an empirically derived, statistically sound scoring system, provided an elderly applicant's age is not assigned a negative factor or value (12 CFR 1002.6(b)(2)(ii)).
  2. Age carries credit information a lender may act on: the length of the applicant's financial history, and the working life left over which a credit is repaid.
  3. In this data the default rate falls with age, in every band (`results/mci_loan/age-check.json`, computed on the same training split, which `prereg2.py` checks):

- **The check on the fitted models.** As in v1, Step 4 reports, per age band on the test split, the mean off-chain margin of BASELINE (now MCI alone) and FULL, and in v2 also the share of each band in each credit tier.
- **Marital status.** `NAME_FAMILY_STATUS` is also a prohibited basis under the same rules. It is one of MCI's candidates, as it was the Bank's in v1; forward selection did not pick it, so no model uses it.
- **Scope.** This is the demo's design choice, argued from widely used rules. The PoC makes no fairness claim and does not assess Iranian lending regulation; a deployment would need the lender's own legal review.

### 5.6 No sensitivity variant in v2

v1's variant gave MCI only its phone and contact columns. It bounded the weakest framing of v1: MCI holding installments_payments as bill and top-up payments. In v2 MCI holds no instalment data, so that variant has no counterpart, and v2 does not run it. The weakest framings of v2 are the partners' (section 5.3). ## 8. Business metrics

v1's two metrics (`docs/mci/02-preregistration.md`, section 8; `scenarios/mci_loan/metrics.py`), computed unchanged, with MCI alone (BASELINE) where v1 had the Bank alone. `v2/metrics2.py` only renames the output keys (`baselineModel`, `atBaselineDefaultRate`, …).

1. **Weights:** each row carries w_c = (population share of class c) / (cohort share of class c) (`metrics.class_weights`).
2. **Primary, the approval rate fixed:** MCI alone approves the rows it predicts as class 0. Its weighted share is the approval rate r. Every model approves the same weighted share r from its lowest margin up. Reported: the weighted default rate among the approved, and its change against MCI alone.

4. **Models:** (a) MCI alone, (b) all members (FULL; FULL_J alongside), (c) all but each partner (LOO_1, LOO_2, LOO_3). (c) shows what each partner's data adds to MCI's decisions.

## 9. Credit policy: calibrated PD and three tiers (new in v2)

MCI decides per subscriber: a 4-month credit, a 1-month credit, or no credit. This layer turns each model's score into that decision (`scenarios/mci_loan/v2/policy.py`). It is off-chain and not ZK-verified: the circuit proves macro-F1 and nothing else.

**The PD.** For each model, on the test rows:
1. **Calibration folds.** Within each class, the test rows in SHA-256("20267007:SK_ID_CURR") order take folds 0 and 1 in turn.
2. **Cross-fitted calibration: never calibrate and report on the same rows** (the student's review, change 2). Calibrate on one half, report on the other, swap, and pool the two reported halves. For the rows of one fold, a logistic regression of TARGET on the margin z₁ − z₀ is fitted by maximum likelihood on the other fold only. The margin is standardized with that fold's mean and SD, and the fit is unweighted, on the balanced rows. It gives p_bal, a default probability in the balanced cohort. Every model gets the same folds and the same procedure. A test checks that no row's PD depends on its own label, or on any other row of its own fold (`scenarios/mci_loan/v2/tests/test_v2.py`, `test_calibration_never_reports_on_its_own_rows`).
3. **Prior correction to application_train's default rate** π = 0.0807: logit PD = logit p_bal + logit π − logit s, where s is the fit fold's default share (about 1/2). This is the standard intercept correction for a sample drawn by class (King and Zeng 2001, *Political Analysis* 9(2)).

**Why the calibrator is fitted this way:**
- **Not on the training rows.** The model was fitted on them, so their margins are more separated than a new subscriber's, and a calibrator fitted there would be overconfident.
- **Cross-fitting.** It keeps every test row's PD out of sample, and needs no new split, so the cohort and the split stay v1's.
- **Two parameters.** Platt scaling has only two, so half of the 3,193 test rows (about 1,600) fits them well.

**Tiers** (the cut-offs approved by the student, section 1, change 2):

| Tier | Persian | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 4-month credit | اعتبار چهارماهه | PD < 4% |
| 1-month credit | اعتبار یک‌ماهه | 4% <= PD < 12% |
| no credit | بدون اعتبار | PD >= 12% |

- **Where the cut-offs come from.** 4% and 12% are 1/2 and 3/2 of π = 8.07%, rounded. The longer, 4-month credit goes only to subscribers estimated at most about half as likely to default as the average applicant. No credit goes to those estimated about 1.5 times as likely or more. Between them, the 1-month credit limits the exposure: a shorter term means a smaller amount outstanding and an earlier sign of trouble.
- **When they were fixed.** Now, from π alone, before any v2 training beyond the smoke run. They were not tuned on any result. The v1 results were seen, but none of them enters the cut-offs.
- **What the PD is.** It is the probability of TARGET = 1 as the dataset defines it. It is not a term-specific default probability: the data has no 1-month or 4-month outcome, so the two credit tiers share one PD and differ only in the cut-off.

**Reported** for each figure, overall and on thin_file. The thin_file rows use the same calibrated PDs. Step 4 gives every number per seed and as a mean over the 10 seeds, from the C2 runs:
- **Per tier:** the rows, the defaults, the weighted share of subscribers, the weighted default rate, and the weighted mean PD.
- **Credit granted** (both credit tiers together): the same figures.
- **The calibration check:** the weighted mean PD against the weighted default rate over all test rows, and each fold's fit.

The comparison asked for is **MCI alone (BASELINE) against the federation (FULL)**. FULL_J and LOO_1..3 are alongside, so the dashboard can show what each partner changes in the tiers.
