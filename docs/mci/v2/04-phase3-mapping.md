# From Phase 3 (Fabric and Go) to the final architecture, v2: MCI as the lender

This maps each element of the Phase 3 prototype to its counterpart in the final architecture, which is the thesis's. The MCI loan instance runs the final architecture unchanged (`docs/mci/00-recon.md`, section 5). In v2, MCI is the lender and the active member (`docs/mci/v2/02-preregistration.md`, tag `mci-prereg-v2`). Each row gives the reason for the change.

**Names** (the naming rule, 2026-10-19). Every partner is a generic simulated role: the Bank, the E-commerce company and the Credit bureau. MCI is the only organisation named. This document refers to the Phase 3 sources' third party as "an e-commerce company", whatever name the sources use.

v1's version of this mapping, with the Bank as the active member, stays in `docs/mci/04-phase3-mapping.md` (tag `mci-v1-bank-active`).

## 1. Who is who

| | Phase 3 | v2 |
|---|---|---|
| Who starts the job | MCI, the INITIATOR (spec:48) | MCI: it creates the run with `FederationFactory.create` and escrows the pool |
| Who holds the label | the Bank, the LABEL_OWNER (spec:122) | MCI: the label is repayment of the credit MCI grants its subscribers |
| Who pays | MCI, the INITIATOR, credits points (spec:104) | MCI: the Talmud awards come from the pool MCI escrowed |
| Data partners | the Bank; an e-commerce company planned as a third (spec:10) | the Bank, the E-commerce company and the Credit bureau, all passive |

**Why the roles merge.** Phase 3 split the job between an initiator (MCI) and a label owner (the Bank). In the final architecture one active party proposes the run, holds the label, trains the fusion model, scores the figures and funds the pool. The other members only send embeddings. The thesis's protocol has one such party per run. In v2 it is MCI, because MCI is the lender: it decides who gets credit and bears the loss when a subscriber does not repay. The Bank becomes a data partner like the others.

## 3. The five Phase 3 functions

| Phase 3 function | What it did | Final contract functions | What changed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `RegisterParticipant(ctx, mspID, role, displayName)` (cc:156) | An organisation registers itself under its MSP ID, with a role. | `FederationFactory.create(Proposal p)` (:45), called by MCI, names every member and role up front. `register(bytes channelKey, address arbiterEcho)` (:406): each partner joins by its own address and posts its channel key and its participation deposit D_p. `acceptArbiter(bytes encKey)` (:422). `commitSeed(bytes32 c)` (:434). `abortSetup()` (:452). | The members are fixed by MCI's proposal before anyone joins. Joining is by the member's own key, against a deposit. A setup that does not complete can be aborted, so no one's funds are stuck. |
| `StartTrainingJob(ctx, jobId, participantsCSV, labelOwnerMSP)` (cc:215) | The initiator opens a job for a list of participants and names the label owner. | `FederationFactory.create` escrows B from MCI and fixes the terms: model-config hash, T, N, maxDepth, verifier, vkHash, deadlines, B, ρ, D_p. The label owner is the proposer itself. Then `commitAlignment(bytes32 gammaI_, uint256 sizeI_, bytes32 gammaA_)` (:475) after the private set intersection, or `resolveAlignmentTimeout()` (:520). Then `revealSeed(uint256 r)` (:496) by every member, which fixes the shared seed, or `resolveRevealFailure()` (:530). | A job becomes a funded run whose terms are committed before training: what will be trained, how it will be scored, and what is paid. The aligned set of subscribers is committed too. Alignment itself was absent in Phase 3, where users were assumed pre-aligned (brief §6). |
| `LogEpochResult(ctx, jobId, epoch)` (cc:300) | The label owner logs each epoch's loss: private value, public hash. | No per-round function. Once, after the last round: `commitFinal(bytes32 root)` (:553) by each partner and `commitFA(bytes32 c, bytes32 gammaG_)` (:567) by MCI. Then the reconciliation window: `proveEquivocation` (:594), `requestResend` (:610), `fileNonReceipt` (:621), `certifyDelivery` (:638), `resolveRelayTimeout` (:652). | The record moves from the label owner's loss to every member's final-round message. It is signed by the sender, so a partner cannot deny it later. Disputes about delivery are settled on-chain. |
| `FinalizeTraining(ctx, jobId, finalModelHash)` (cc:401) | The label owner marks the job finished and records a model hash. | `finalizeReconciliation()` (:664), then `submitScore(uint8 kind, uint8 memberIndex, uint256 F, bytes32 cModel, uint256[8] proof)` (:693) and `submitJointScore(uint256 F, uint256[8] proof)` (:684), by MCI. Each of the six figures (F_r, F_j, LOO_1..3, BASELINE) is accepted only with a valid Groth16 proof. `resolveScoringTimeout()` (:738) ends a run whose figures never arrive. | "Finished" used to be the label owner's word. Now it means proven performance figures, bound to the committed model and data. MCI is the one who pays, so the proofs protect the partners from an MCI that would understate their contributions. The v2 runs had every figure accepted (`results/mci_loan/v2/onchain/runs.csv`). |
| `DistributeRewards(ctx, jobId)` (cc:448) | The initiator credits points to every participant, once. | `settlementDivision()` (:754): E, E′, l, a by `SettlementMath`. `acknowledge()` (:775). `objectAlignment(bytes sigA, bytes32 listHash)` (:951). `settleBackup()` (:963). `withdraw()` (:820). | Points become tokens from the pool MCI escrowed, divided by the Talmud rule on the proven leave-one-out claims. The once-only guard of Phase 3 survives as the settlement path, which is set once, and as pull-payment withdrawals. In the v2 runs the tokens were conserved (`results/mci_loan/v2/onchain/runs.csv`, `tokens_conserved`). |

## 4. What did not change

- **The audit trail.** Every lifecycle step is a transaction with an event, readable by every member. The dashboard's audit page reads `results/mci_loan/v2/onchain/transactions.csv` and `events.csv`.
- **Timestamps.** They come from the chain (block numbers), never from a member's clock, as in spec §2.4.
- **Errors.** A wrong caller or a wrong state is rejected with an explicit, testable error (custom errors in `Federation.sol`). This continues spec §2.6 and the Phase 3 test categories:
  - access control;
  - state machine;
  - privacy (only commitments on-chain);
  - idempotent settlement.
